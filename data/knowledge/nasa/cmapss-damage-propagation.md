# Damage Propagation Modeling for Aircraft Engine Run-to-Failure Simulation

> **Provenance.** A summary of the publicly available NASA technical paper by
> Saxena, Goebel, Simon and Eklund that accompanies the C-MAPSS dataset. The
> original is distributed with the dataset as `Damage Propagation Modeling.pdf`.

## Dataset construction

The C-MAPSS turbofan degradation datasets are generated by a thermodynamic
simulation of a large commercial turbofan. Each trajectory represents one engine
run from an initial, healthy condition through to functional failure.

Engines begin with varying degrees of initial wear and manufacturing variation.
This variation is considered normal and is not treated as a fault condition.
Because each engine starts in a slightly different condition, deterioration must
be assessed relative to the individual engine's own initial state rather than to
a fleet-wide reference.

## Fault modes

| Subset | Operating conditions | Fault modes |
|---|---|---|
| FD001 | 1 | HPC degradation |
| FD002 | 6 | HPC degradation |
| FD003 | 1 | HPC degradation and fan degradation |
| FD004 | 6 | HPC degradation and fan degradation |

Degradation is injected as a gradual loss of flow capacity and efficiency in the
affected module. The onset is gradual and the growth is monotonic.

## Operating conditions

The six-condition subsets cycle through combinations of altitude, Mach number and
throttle resolver angle. These conditions change the magnitude of every sensor
reading substantially — by considerably more than a full lifetime of
deterioration changes it. Any analysis that does not account for operating
condition will measure the flight condition rather than the engine's health.

## Remaining useful life

Remaining useful life is defined as the number of remaining operational cycles
before functional failure. In the test sets, the trajectory ends some time before
failure and the remaining life at that point is supplied separately.

It is common practice to cap the RUL target at a constant value early in life,
because degradation is negligible before onset and the sensor data does not
distinguish a very healthy engine from a merely healthy one.

## Evaluation

An asymmetric scoring function is used in place of a symmetric error measure.
Late predictions — those that overestimate remaining life — are penalised more
heavily than early predictions, because an engine believed healthier than it is
will be dispatched and may strand an aircraft.
